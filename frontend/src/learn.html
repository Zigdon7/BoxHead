Learn to Play

Go in. Get loot.
Get out alive.

BoxHead is a co-op zombie extraction shooter. Bring gear into a raid, fill your pack and reach an extraction point before the clock runs out. Make it out and you keep everything. Die and you lose what you brought.

The loop

01 / Hideout

Gear up

Your stash holds everything you own. Equip a primary and a secondary gun, armor and a pack. Sell spare loot to the trader, who sells basic gear up to power 24, and save valuables needed for Homefront upgrades. Before you deploy you can insure your loadout (starting at 35% of its sale value): if the raid is lost, your equipped gear, including the pack itself, comes back. Pack contents, bounty, entry fee and the premium don't.

02 / Raid

Loot the city

Pick a map you have the gear score for, then drop in. Open supply crates inside buildings, take loot from monsters and avoid lingering near nests. Kills add to a cash bounty.

03 / Extract

Make it out

Stand inside a green extraction point for 6 seconds. Your loadout, pack and bounty go back to the hideout. If you are killed, the raid clock runs out or you leave, everything you carried in is lost.

New operators start with a Service Pistol, Padded Vest, Canvas Satchel and $400. With nothing equipped you can still raid Outskirts with a loaner pistol and a 2-slot pack, so you can never get stuck. It's weaker than any real kit. Your stash holds 40 items besides what you have equipped; if it is full when you extract, the loot that doesn't fit is sold for you.

Power and rarity

Gear score decides where you can go

Every piece of gear has a power from 1 to 100. Your gear score is the average power of your four slots (an empty slot counts as 0). Rarer items roll extra power on top of the map's loot band and sell for much more.

Four maps, one ladder

Bigger maps, tougher enemies, better loot

Outskirts is free; harder maps need a minimum gear score and charge an entry fee, paid even if you die. Each map's common loot stops just short of the next map's gate; rarity bonuses (+3 to +15 power) push the best finds over it. The base trader sells up to power 24, enough for Rust Yard. Armory upgrades add stronger stock, but reaching Quarantine Zone and Black Site still requires gear extracted from raids.

Field operations

Inside a raid

Raids are persistent: other players may already be inside, and each operator has their own raid clock from the moment they deploy. Squadmates who deploy within 30 seconds of each other share an insertion point. The radar (bottom right) shows the area around you; press M for the full map.

Threat recognition

What lives out there

Every enemy attacks in melee. Their health and damage scale with the map, and on harder maps some spawn as elites: bigger, tougher, ringed in gold and far more likely to drop gear. If everywhere you've been in the last 30 seconds is within 14 m of where you are now for 75 seconds, the nests send hunters after you: first an Abomination, then packs of elites for as long as you keep camping (hunters pay only their base bounty). The raid header warns you from 45 seconds and counts down; travelling drains the meter.

Personal arsenal

Two guns. One very sharp backup.

Base damage is before gun power and critical hits (1.75×). Guns other than the pistol share your ammo reserve. Switch with 1 and 2.

Shape the battlefield

Deployables

Your pack sets how many you start with, and enemy drops and combos give you more. Place them with E/Q/F or 5/6/7. Explosives chain-react.

Survive together

Squad rules & survival tricks

Bring them back

A downed player bleeds out after 25 seconds. Stand next to them for 3 seconds to revive them with half their health. With nobody else alive in the raid, you have only 3 seconds before you die.

Chain your kills

Kill again within 4 seconds to keep your combo going. Each step adds 20% to your kill bounty, up to 3×, and every fifth kill resupplies you. A 10-kill combo triggers Overdrive: faster movement and attacks.

Full pack?

With a full pack, gear only replaces weaker carried gear. Valuables and sealed items compete by sale value or estimated opened value. Press I to inspect your pack and drop a chosen item, or G to drop its last item. The raid continues while the pack is open. Gear you find can be equipped after you extract.

Pausing

You can pause with P only while you are alone in a raid. It freezes everything, raid clock included, and anyone who joins resumes it.

Leaving is losing

Leaving or disconnecting mid-raid forfeits your loadout and loot, just like dying. If the server restarts during your raid, the raid is voided: your gear, entry fee and insurance premium are returned.

Between raids

Operators, Homefront and seasons

Gear gets you into harder maps. These four systems are the long game: they make each raid cheaper, safer or more profitable, and they are how an account grows from season to season.

Operators

Up to three characters

An account holds up to three operators. Each has its own cash, stash, loadout, Homefront and Operations ranks. Create and switch them in the hideout's Operators tab; deleting one frees its slot and earns nothing. Every new operator is Safe for now: Raidable operators arrive with PvP.

Homefront

Your base, tiers 1 to 4

Raise the tier with cash and valuables from your stash: tier 2 costs $1,500 and 2 uncommon-or-better valuables, tier 3 $6,000 and 3 rare-or-better, tier 4 $20,000 and 3 epic-or-better. Safe operators stop at tier 3. A facility's level can't pass the tier:

Base

Build it, test it, defend it

The hideout's Base tab is a tower-defense map. Put your HQ on a side or corner, then place walls, gun turrets, mines and garrison soldiers; every entrance must keep a way to the HQ. Set up three waves of soldiers and run a practice raid on your own base for free. Where base raids are switched on, Raidable operators can scout another base through fog of war and send their waves in for a share of its cash.

Operations

A rank on every map

Every raid earns Operations XP on its map: 100 for extracting, 25 for a lost raid, plus 1 per kill (up to 100). Ranks come at 150, 500, 1,200, 2,500 and 5,000 XP. Each rank cuts that map's entry fee by 10% and adds 4% to the bounty you bank there.

Seasons & Legacy

Retire for permanent perks

A season is a calendar quarter. In its last four weeks you may retire one operator whose Homefront is tier 3 or higher, once per season. The operator and everything it owns are gone. The account keeps up to 3 Legacy points (by what the operator was worth: $5,000, $20,000, $50,000), 5 more stash slots, a season badge and a head start next season (+5% bounty and +10% Operations XP, growing up to three times that if you retire in back-to-back seasons). Retiring is optional, and skipping a season costs nothing.

Each perk has 3 levels, a level costs 1 Legacy point, and an account can earn 12 points in all. Perks apply to every operator you own.

Insurance never costs less than 15% of your gear's value, however many Office levels and Broker ranks you have.

Signing in

Your account

Sign in to play

There is no guest play: your operators and stash live on your account, on any device. Sign in with Steam, Bluesky or an email address and password (and with Google, where the server offers it). Once signed in you can link Steam and Bluesky to the same account from the home screen.

Confirm your email

If you sign up with an email address, we send a confirmation link (valid for 3 days). You can look around the hideout straight away, but you can't deploy or host until you follow it. Accounts that also sign in through Steam, Bluesky or Google skip this.

Who you meet

You are matched with players who signed in the same way you did this session: Steam, Bluesky, or email and Google. Turn on cross-platform play on the deploy screen to share one pool with everyone else who turned it on.

Reset or delete

Forgot your password? Ask for a reset link on the sign-in screen; it lasts an hour and signs out every other device. You can delete your account, with every operator and stash, from the home screen under Account & linked sign-ins.
